title: "Knowledge is the fast food of education"
date: 2026-11-08

---

Knowledge is not confined to classes, books, and lecturers, which readily feed the learner ready-made answers for consumption without the effortful process of intuition leading to discovery. Knowledge abounds even in the mundane. However, one must cultivate an intuition for *how* to look for knowledge.

One can analyze an everyday conversation and dissect the implicit linguistic, social, and nonverbal conventions. One can look at a fork and debate its natural teleology, the history of its evolution, its metallic composition, its manufacturing process. Dismissing the observed for a presumed lack of knowledge to be gained from it is not an inadequacy of the observed; it is the inadequacy of the observer for lacking the skill to observe. [[everything is a data point of reality|Everything is a data point of reality.]]

That one can foster this ability, too, is far more rewarding, integrative, and indicative of cognitive depth than merely consuming prepackaged knowledge from conventional sources. The student of life, who finds study in all reality, has far more profundity than the student who confines study to the classroom, books, experts, etc.

Hence, intuition (in how to look for knowledge) creates quantity and quality of knowledge to be consumed, which in turn creates more intuition (in ideas to be validated).

It also augments the ability to synthesize: consumption of knowledge in traditional sources is easy due to the fluency heuristic, and often results in silos. Consumption of knowledge in nontraditional sources is an active practice of dismantling silos. This is the quintessence of synthesis.

Hence, if one is to produce knowledge, one must realize this: All that is now known was once intuition. [[intuition-transcends-knowledge|Beyond the fast food of knowledge, strive for the deep nourishment of intuition.]]
