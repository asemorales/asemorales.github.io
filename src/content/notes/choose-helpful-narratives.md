---
title: "Choose helpful narratives"
date: 2025-11-30
updated: 2026-11-07
tags: ["cognition","philosophy"]
---

The narratives we tell ourselves do not capture the infinite complexity of reality, and they may not always be empirically provable even if they can be arrived at through rationality. Yet does that really matter? If those narratives are more conducive than not to a human being and to humanity, then the narratives are useful tools. Useful [discretizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretization) of an infinitely complex and continuous reality[^1].

The unhelpful, torturous narratives are often just as true as the helpful, refined narratives. The jaded, godforsaken narrative a survivor makes in the middle of trauma is just as true as the healing, hopeful narrative a survivor makes after years of processing and acceptance. Yet we have the agency to choose our narratives. Choose the helpful ones.

Human capacity and the nature of humans as narrative-reliant beings may only be able to comprehend strands of thread, sometimes interconnecting and entangled, but ultimately strands. Through strands we see attenuated, focused glimpses of the abstract and formless bundled possibilities of reality which we can only strive to wholly comprehend[^2]. And so, we choose to make helpful narratives. For narratives, even if incomplete, are how we make sense of our humanity. Make and choose the helpful ones.

[^1]: Narratives are, in some sense, a form of [reductionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductionism). While reductionism does not adequately describe complexity, it has historically been an extremely useful paradigm to understand elementary ideas that have led us to understand and have the tools to describe complexity today.

[^2]: That is, in creating narratives as discretizations of complex reality, there is inevitably some [discretization error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretization_error). However, in choosing our narratives, we can also choose the amount and nature of error. Hence, choose one that not only minimizes overall error, but is most useful compromise between the reader and the writer.
