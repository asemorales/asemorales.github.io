The Roman god [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_(mythology)) was both the messenger of the gods and the god of commerce, and historically, commerce and communication are closely related activities. In Latin, *commercium* refers to both trade and correspondence (i.e. *commercium epistularum* refers to an exchange of letters).

That is, commerce and communication are both fundamentally activities of exchange of information[^1]. Similarly, currency and messengers are both mediums of exchange. Both are also activities of translation: money translates diverse goods into a common measure of value, and messages translate thoughts into words and actions.

Currency, then, is similar to a language that enables conversation between strangers, allowing them to negotiate and reach agreement across cultural and geographical boundaries.

[^1]: While commerce is also an exchange of goods, I refer to commerce as an exchange of information here in the sense that commerce is tied to [price signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_signal).
