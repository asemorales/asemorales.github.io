>[!quote] Richard Feynman
>What I cannot create, I do not understand.

The main mechanism of learning is not the addition or formation of brain cells (nodes), but the formation, pruning, myelination of neurons (edges) in one's nervous system (network)[^2]. Hence, it is useful both organizationally and cognitively to [prefer associative ontologies to hierarchical taxonomies](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z8SU3r8xyZyvwRhyDdJasJ2).

This explains why knowledge is consumed on a day-to-day basis[^1], but is often not remembered. Before any mechanism of memory, most knowledge has not been learned. The formation of neurons is necessary for long-term retention, hence the value of [incremental writing](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_writing) and [learning by writing](https://www.cold-takes.com/learning-by-writing/) as activities that cultivate the formation of connections. [[Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not Work An Analysis of the Failure of Constructivist, Discovery, Problem-Based, Experiential, and Inquiry-Based Teaching|If nothing has been created, nothing has been learned.]]

This also explains why insights arrived at through [[intuition-transcends-knowledge|intuition transcends knowledge]], because these insights often require conscious effort and connections to arrive at, as compared to knowledge that is merely consumed. [[knowledge-as-fast-food|Knowledge is the fast food of education]].

This makes a good case for [[tech-to-automate-input|using technology to automate input of ideas, and manually processing insights]].
