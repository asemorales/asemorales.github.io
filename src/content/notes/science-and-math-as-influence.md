It seems to be a common belief that science and mathematics are more free of influence games[^4] than other careers and endeavors. Not only is this a misconception, but I argue that science and mathematics are *more* of an influence game than other careers, albeit a *different* kind. That is,

>Science and mathematics are the only unbounded influence games.

***

## How are science and mathematics unbounded influence games?

**Measures of scientific performance.** In modern science and mathematics, performance is measured by citations, $h$-index, and several other metrics. By definition, a [citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation) is a unit of influence. That is, a citation is a article-level metric, wherein one citation corresponds to that article influencing another article. The [h-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-index) is also a unit of influence. That is, the $h$-index is an [author-level metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Author-level_metric), wherein $h$ is the largest $h$ such that $h$ of the author's articles have at least $h$ citations each. While both have their limitations (as all metrics do[^1][^2]), both make clear that the measure of a scientific[^3] work or author is their influence.

**Within and across organizations.** Often, in other careers, promotion or *[de facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto_and_de_jure)* power are based on your influence *within* the organization (i.e. influence over coworkers, employees, manager, etc.), and your influence over external organizations is often based on your influence within yours. Promotions and job titles are chosen on the basis of your impact *to the organization*, hence why it is far easier to move up in smaller organizations such as startups rather than larger organizations.

In science and mathematics, however, your influence is not limited to an organization, nor is it even limited to your lifetime, but to your influence over *cumulative human knowledge*. That is, rather than influence to perhaps 10-500 or 1000+ people in your organization, or to over a million customers who use your company product, scientific and mathematical impact is unbounded. Many influential thinkers did not see their life's work generally accepted within their lifetime, including [Mendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Mendel) and [Semmelweis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignaz_Semmelweis) to name a few. In any other career, they would have been considered unsuccessful, and died believing so.

**Conceptual foundations and cumulative human knowledge.** Science and mathematics are highly subject to the [Matthew effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_effect). Given that science and mathematics are cumulative human endeavors, your work is built upon for an unbounded amount of time. This is in comparison to other fields. In art, for example, you may be a highly influential artist who inspired the works of others, but their works are not required to credit or reference yours within the content; the idea of influence suffices. The same applies to engineering. However, in science and mathematics, the output is the idea itself, and hence the ideas that influenced are held highly.

**Academic lineage.** Similarly, the Matthew effect applies to academic lineage. The highest prestige in modern science usually comes in the form of winning a [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize), [Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal)[^5], or [Turing award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award). Of these, both the Nobel and Turing awardees overwhelmingly come from either the same or similar academic lineage[^cite-tol-nobel][^cite-changfu-fields][^8].

***

## Why do we think that science and mathematics are free of influence games?

**Influence based on correctness, politics, and both.** Influence based on correctness is an appeal to truth (ideas), while influence based on politics is an appeal to power (people). In practice, influence is often based on both. However, power is ephemeral[^9], whereas truth is not. When the latter fails within one's lifetime, there is no reappeal. The dead no longer have power. When the former fails, the idea can still resurface. Science and mathematics must have true foundations in order to progress, hence there is a selection pressure toward truth that allows for rediscovery.

**Influence within human lifetimes.** It follows, then, that this view stems from the limitations of what we can observe within our human lifetimes. Science and mathematics are the only unbounded influence games, and yet less visible ones, because their influence extends beyond our lifetimes.
