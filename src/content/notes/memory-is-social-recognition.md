Underlying the concept of memory is an implicit assumption that memory is social recognition. Memory is often less about truth and more about acknowledgement.

It manifests in our concept of leaving legacies as social artifacts. People often seem to fear non-recognition after non-existence more than they fear either losing their actual existence to entropy or never having manifested an artifact into existence at all.

It manifests in survivorship bias through historical writing, businesses and economies, evolution, the anthropic principle, and so on.

It manifests in metrics, which are frozen snapshots of past states. Work "matters" insofar as it drives meaningful metrics.

It manifests in shame as self-erasure: if one is not remembered for a negative background, one can redefine their identity.

It manifests in one's own memory: forgetting is a lack of social recognition by oneself.

Why is it that memory is measured by social recognition rather than the form of reality? In much the same manner that dried riverbeds remember the streams that flowed through them, that carvings remember the motions of a sculptor, that economies lock in historically influential yet inferior technologies, that critical transitions remember every prior condition leading to the final significant perturbation, that mathematical proofs are fossils of every axiom and lemma leading to their formulation... existence and non-existence are simultaneously and universally remembered by the shape of reality, regardless of whatever name it takes: hysteresis, path dependence, chaos theory, eligibility trace, computational irreducibility, and so on.

Perhaps one ought not worry about remembrance. Being remembered is the inconsequential baseline, for the geometry of reality will remember regardless. Each event, by constraining the set of possible future states, is necessarily encoded. Entropy encodes history in irreversibility.

By virtue of truth, entropic memory outweighs social memory[^1]. Therefore, the question of "What do I want to be remembered for?" mistakes social recognition for ontological persistence. To ask how one will be remembered rests on a mistaken definition of an observer. A more reality-grounded question would be to ask, "What constraints on the future do I want my existence to impose?"
