---
title: "Abstraction layers are techniques to handle complexity"
date: 2026-11-05
updated: 2026-11-08
tags: ["complexity","science","mathematics","engineering"]
---

In some forms of [emergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergence)[^1], the higher-level domain exhibits new laws that differ from the lower-level domain. To study the laws of the higher-level domain, new fields of study are created. Hence, the new field of study is a technique to handle complexity in science and mathematics. These higher-level fields of study are [abstraction layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstraction_(computer_science)) over the field below it[^2].

In computing (and more broadly, engineering), an abstraction is often thought of as *leaving things out*: i.e. creating a higher-level implementation that hides the details of implementation. Hence, it becomes more manageable for people to work with the higher-level implementation. In practice, however, an abstraction also *involves new rules* to operate at that layer. For example, programming in [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++) (the higher-level domain) requires that you program by the syntax and practices of C++, not by the rules of [assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language) (the lower-level domain).

Hence, in science, mathematics, and engineering, abstraction layers are techniques to handle complexity. Where, they differ, however, is how rules come about. In science, rules of higher abstraction layers *arise* out of complexity, while in engineering, abstraction layers are *created* to manage complexity. In mathematics, whether rules arise or are created depends on one's response to the debate of whether mathematics is discovered or created.

[^1]: By some forms of emergence, I refer to [[glossary#strong emergence (noun)|strong emergence]] and [[glossary#weak emergence (noun)|robust weak emergence]], since not all weakly emergent phenomenon result in new laws in the higher-level domain.

[^2]: Abstraction layers are, indirectly, methods of influence over higher layers. This is related to [[science-and-math-as-influence|science and mathematics as the only unbounded influence games.]]
