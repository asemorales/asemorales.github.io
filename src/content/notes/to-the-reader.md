> [!quote] Michel de Montaigne, *[Essays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essays_%28Montaigne%29)*
> I myself am the subject of my book: it is not reasonable that you should employ your leisure on a topic so frivolous and so vain.

In a word, this site is an autobiography. It is an experiment in creating [living documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_document) of [[glossary#entelechy (noun)|entelechy]][^6] within myself.

By that, I mean to say that I am a [computationally irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_irreducibility) [complex system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_system). At any given moment I am simultaneously in a certain state, and unfolding towards future possible states[^1] within the adjacent possible. I am unable to predict my future states, although I am guilty for having had the intellectual arrogance to try and do so for much of my life. I am guilty of planning rather than experimenting.

Hence, I am trying a new approach, from which this website was born. Instead of planning my life ahead, my writings herein are an experiment in documenting who I am by articulating my current ideas, and allowing my ideas to unfold. I am my my ideas, and thus [[the evolution of ideas are biographies]][^5]. Hence, this site is an autobiography.

## About the Author

I might want to tell you about myself, but in truth I am probably fundamentally unable to tell you. Any narrative I give in an "About the author" page is a distilled mental model I have about myself at my current state. That narrative would be both inaccurate (for my mental model of myself is biased) and incomplete (for in describing only my current state I deprive you of the opportunity to observe greater complexity and past states). Hence I have decided not to hand you my self-model through some limited narrative, and instead invite you to create your own narrative, your own mental model of me, from my writing. This entire site is "About the Author."

## A Writer's Apology

> [!quote] G. H. Hardy, *[A Mathematician's Apology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Mathematician's_Apology)*
> I have never done anything ‘useful’. No discovery of mine has made, or is likely to make, directly or indirectly, for good or ill, the least difference to the amenity of the world. [...] I have just one chance of escaping a verdict of complete triviality, that I may be judged to have created something worth creating.

If any part of these writings are lacking context, or difficult to understand, or so on, I apologize in advance. I write in whatever manner is natural to me, and little effort has been made to translate my writing for a general public. I write primarily for myself[^2] as an exercise in rigor to develop my thoughts, for [[rigor develops precision]]. It is also a memory aid for when my future self will forget me, for writing is an extension of memory[^3]. Put briefly: my writing is an entirely selfish[^4] endeavor.

[^1]: > [!quote] Buckminster Fuller, *[I Seem to be a Verb](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL21115131M/I_seem_to_be_a_verb._--.)*
> I live on Earth at present, and I don't know what I am. I know that I am not a category. I am not a thing—a noun. I seem to be a verb, an evolutionary process—an integral function of the universe.

[^2]: > [!quote] Oscar Wilde, *[The Soul of Man Under Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Soul_of_Man_Under_Socialism)*
> The moment that an artist takes notice of what other people want, and tries to supply the demand, he ceases to be an artist, and becomes a dull or an amusing craftsman, an honest or a dishonest tradesman. He has no further claim to be considered as an artist.

[^3]: [[memory-is-social-recognition|Memory is primarily social recognition.]]

[^4]: Why is it that selfish entails a negative connotation, even in cases when prioritizing the self does not cause detriment to others? I much prefer to call it a self-seeking endeavor.

[^5]: [[why-do-we-define-ourselves-by-what-we-do-rather-than-what-we-think|Why do we define ourselves by what we do, rather than what we think?]]

[^6]: I could have described this as an experiment in self-improvement, or self-actualization, or change, but that would be sufficiently precise. Many present-day definitions of self-improvement and self-actualization seem to have an underlying assumption that the current self is insufficient, hence the need for self-improvement. This is closer to Aristotle's definition of kinesis rather than entelechia. I use entelechy in the sense that it not only describes change, but also self-completeness in every possible state. If I were to define entelechy against self-actualization, it would be more akin to this: I am in the process of self-actualization, and simultaneously I am complete at every moment: before, toward, and during self-actualization.
